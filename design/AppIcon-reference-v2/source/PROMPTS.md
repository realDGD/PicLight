# Image generation prompts

Mode: built-in `image_gen.imagegen`. All calls requested a transparent background. The user's uploaded image was a visual reference; embedded labels were not treated as instructions.

## Native foreground

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a single transparent foreground artwork layer for a production macOS app icon, square 1024x1024 canvas. Input image is ONLY a visual reference; create ONE clean isolated white cartoon glove holding a silver-white flashlight, closely following the beautiful large light-mode icon on the LEFT of the reference. Retain its thick dark-navy outlines, soft cool blue dimensional shading, pill-shaped amber yellow switch, pale yellow elliptical lamp glass, big rounded glove silhouette with just three curved finger contours. The back of the glove faces the viewer, fingers wrap around the lower flashlight like the reference, do not add exposed fingers across the front. Composition should match the LEFT reference's icon interior at full scale: a diagonal flashlight points up-right about 37 degrees right of vertical, its elliptical lens centered near (615,370) and rim spanning approximately x480..735,y270..480; silver head ends near (485,565); switch at (410,570); glove occupies bottom-left from x0..475,y555..1024 with wrist reaching beyond left and bottom edges. Emphasize large simple readable silhouette, elegant smooth shapes and correct grip; do not simplify to a thin line icon. NO blue tile or rounded-square enclosure, NO background, NO cast shadow beyond the subject, NO emitted beam/cone or outer glow, NO text, NO contact sheet, NO duplicate objects. The empty upper right must be genuinely transparent so a separate native beam and system Liquid Glass tile can be added later. The artwork is dimensional illustration but not a photographed object. Keep the charming polished reference character.

## Export notes

These prompts describe the requested outcomes, not guarantees of pixel identity. The generated transparent margins retained artifacts. `ReferenceExport.icon` uses Apple's standard icon mask to produce the final clean contour; `build-icons.py` exports, adds traditional macOS canvas padding, and packages the PNGs into ICNS without redrawing their interiors. Native material previews are separately rendered from `PicLight.icon`.
